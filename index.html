1. Real-Time Riverine Flood Monitoring and Early Warning System for the Lower Shire (Work in progress)

Problem

Communities in the Lower Shire Valley of Malawi are extremely vulnerable to recurrent flooding that affects over 1,500 households. Existing national solutions and satellite systems (such as Google Flood Hub) fail to capture localized, ground- level hydrological conditions accurately. Without localized early detection, residents receive little to no advance warning, resulting in chaotic emergency evacuations, loss of human life, and destruction of property.

What Is Being Done

Designing and developing an off-grid, solar-powered embedded early warning system using an ESP32 microcontroller. Building firmware incorporating noise-reduction filtering and three-tier threshold alert logic (Advisory, Observe, Warning). Integrating multi-channel alerting capable of delivering SMS notifications to local residents in Chichewa, triggering local sirens and strobe lights, and logging live data to a web-based dashboard with historical trend analysis for disaster authorities.

Tools In Use

  • Hardware: ESP32 Development Board, HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Water Level Sensor, TSW-20M Turbidity Sensor, SIM800L GSM Module, 6W Solar Panel, 18650 Li-ion Battery Pack, LM2596 Voltage Regulator, TP4056 Battery Charger, Active Buzzer, LEDs, MOSFET.
  • Software: Arduino IDE, Proteus, Visual Studio Code.

2. Server Room Monitoring System

Problem

Server rooms host critical IT infrastructure vulnerable to environmental hazards such as elevated temperature, high humidity, smoke, and grid power outages. Unmonitored environmental spikes lead to hardware degradation, catastrophic equipment failure, and costly operational downtime.

What Was Done

Simulated and prototyped an automated monitoring unit built around a Raspberry Pi 3 single-board computer. Developed a modular Python software architecture to poll environmental sensors, drive local actuators (fan cooling relay, buzzer alarm, status LEDs, I2C LCD), and execute a state machine for switching power to battery backup during grid outages. Built a cloud-backed PostgreSQL database on Supabase to log telemetry via HTTP REST and rendered a web dashboard with user authentication for remote administration.

Tools Used

  • Hardware: Raspberry Pi 3 SBC, DHT22 Temperature & Humidity Sensor, MQ-2 Gas/Smoke Sensor, I2C 16x2 LCD Display, Dual Relay Modules, DC Fan & Load Motors, Red/Green Status LEDs, Active Buzzer, 9V Backup Battery, Voltage Dividers, PEF Board.
  • Software: Thonny IDE, Wokwi Simulator, VS Code, Supabase PostgreSQL.

How It Went

The physical prototype passed fault testing across all target conditions. Exceeding temperature limits engaged the cooling fan within 5 seconds and sounded local alarms. Removing grid power triggered seamless switching to the 9V battery within the debounce window, and power restoration safely waited out a 15- second stabilization period before reconnecting grid supply to protect relays. Telemetry posted reliably to the cloud every 30 seconds.

What To Do Differently

  • Replace HTTP polling on the web dashboard with Supabase Realtime subscriptions for lower latency, push-based alerts
  • Integrate SMS or email notifications (via Twilio or Supabase Edge Functions) to alert administrators instantly off-site during emergency events.

3. Automatic Greenhouse Monitoring System

Problem

Manually managing crop growth factors such as temperature and soil moisture inside a greenhouse is labor-intensive and inaccurate. Inconsistent environmental control negatively affects crop health, yields, and water efficiency.

What Was Done

Designed, simulated and assembled a greenhouse model to maintain temperature and soil moisture levels automatically. Programmed an Arduino UNO microcontroller to continuously monitor soil moisture and temperature. Automated a DC water pump to irrigate soil when dry, operated a DC exhaust fan and motorized roof vents (using servo motors) to vent heat when temperatures rose, and transmitted real-time metrics directly to the farmer's smartphone.

Tools Used

  • Hardware: Arduino UNO Microcontroller, DS18B20 Water-Resistant Temperature Sensor, Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor, Relay Modules, 5V DC Water Pump, DC Cooling Fan, Servo Motors, Active Buzzer, HC-05 Bluetooth Module.
  • Software: Arduino IDE, Cirkit Designer IDE.

How It Went

The prototype successfully automated climate regulation. When internal temperature reached 30°C or higher, the fan engaged and servo motors automatically opened the greenhouse roof vents to reduce heat. The capacitive sensor correctly triggered the water pump upon detecting dry soil, and sensor values streamed continuously to the mobile app over Bluetooth.

What To Do Differently

  • Replace the short-range HC-05 Bluetooth module with Wi-Fi or cellular/5G connectivity to permit long-distance remote monitoring when the farmer is far from the greenhouse.

Understanding SCADA Systems

1. What is a SCADA System?

Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems represent an essential integration of industrial hardware and software engineered to capture Operational Technology (OT) real-time data and automate complex processes. In modern industrial facilities, SCADA serves as the vital digital bridge connecting physical field equipment such as electric motors, pumps, sensors, and valves to centralized monitoring servers or edge platforms. By giving operators continuous visibility into machine operations and automated supervisory capabilities, SCADA empowers organizations to streamline workflows, optimize resource consumption, and minimize unplanned operational downtime across both local plants and geographically dispersed infrastructure.

At its core, a SCADA system fulfills four crucial business functions: controlling processes locally or remotely, capturing and analyzing real-time field data, enabling direct operator interaction with physical machinery, and archiving historical events for report generation and predictive compliance. Depending on the architecture, engineers can inspect system performance from an operator workstation overlooking a factory floor, an industrial Human-Machine Interface (HMI) mounted directly beside machinery, or remotely through secure enterprise network platforms.

2. SCADA System Hardware and Architecture

A physical SCADA ecosystem relies on a hierarchy of specialized computing hardware designed to survive harsh industrial environments while acquiring telemetry from field instruments. Primary sensors measure variables like temperature, pressure, fluid level, and vibration, passing signals to local execution hardware such as Remote Terminal Units (RTUs) and Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs). PLCs and RTUs execute programmed automation logic directly at the site, triggering local actuators to adjust valves or toggle machinery based on real-time readings.

In modern SCADA architectures, raw sensor data collected by PLCs is aggregated by an industrial computer functioning as a gateway. Gateways pass telemetry from PLCs upstream to centralized servers or cloud environments. Edge computers situated close to data sources can also function as gateways while performing local processing on telemetry prior to cloud transmission. Processing workloads at the edge enables faster local decision-making, drastically reduces network bandwidth demands, and lowers cloud storage costs.

Local operator interaction is facilitated through Human-Machine Interfaces (HMIs), touchscreen computing units that display live schematics, equipment controls, and system alarms directly next to machinery. Downstream, a central SCADA server functions as the supervisory master unit. This server hosts the local historian database, which records time-series data over months or years, establishing the historical baseline needed for continuous optimization and trend analysis.

3. Real-World Applications

Power Grid Automation by ESCOM: The Electricity Supply Corporation of Malawi (ESCOM) relies on SCADA technology to achieve real-time supervisory control and data acquisition across national electrical generation, transmission, and distribution networks. Through SCADA-connected Remote Terminal Units installed across hydro stations, major substations, and distribution transformers, ESCOM power dispatchers monitor line voltages, current loads, grid frequency, and transformer temperatures from central control rooms. When a lightning strike or short circuit occurs along a high-voltage transmission line, the SCADA system automatically detects the electrical fault, communicates with local protection relays, and isolates the damaged grid segment within milliseconds. This automated isolation protects multi-million-dollar substation transformers from catastrophic failure and enables ESCOM dispatchers to reroute power through healthy lines, reducing load shedding impact and shortening power restoration times for residential and commercial customers.

Industrial Automation by Alliance One: Alliance One Tobacco utilizes advanced SCADA and PLC control architectures to automate large-scale agricultural leaf processing, conditioning, and drying operations. Within processing facilities, SCADA software coordinates a vast network of field sensors, conveyor drives, steam valves, and thermal regulation units across curing and conditioning lines. Precise temperature and moisture control is mandatory during leaf processing to maintain strict export quality standards. SCADA systems continuously sample humidity and thermal levels inside processing chambers, instructing PLCs to adjust steam injection valves and conveyor speeds automatically in response to micro-fluctuations. Furthermore, SCADA tracks automated weighing stations, providing plant managers with real-time throughput metrics, minimizing raw material waste, and facilitating predictive maintenance on heavy mechanical equipment to avoid costly processing bottlenecks during peak harvest seasons.

Municipal Water Treatment and Distribution by Blantyre Water Board (BWB): Blantyre Water Board uses SCADA infrastructure to automate the extraction, treatment, and distribution of potable water from the Shire River at Walker's Ferry to the city of Blantyre. SCADA systems continually monitor raw water intake pumps, chemical dosing rates, filter beds, and high-pressure booster stations. When river turbidity spikes after heavy rainfall, the SCADA network automatically adjusts coagulation dosing meters and regulates intake valve positions to maintain water safety standards while avoiding pump cavitation. Real-time telemetry across booster stations and service reservoirs ensures balanced hydraulic pressure across high- elevation distribution zones, preventing pipe bursts and detecting major distribution line leaks instantly.

4. Hybrid Control and Step-by-Step SCADA Implementation

Modern SCADA deployments offer flexible hybrid control frameworks that balance automated rule execution with human oversight. Facilities can program systems to automatically handle emergency shutdowns during critical threshold breaches, while sending cautionary alerts for minor anomalies to human operators, who can then make informed operational decisions.

To successfully implement a SCADA solution, engineering teams should follow a structured roadmap: begin by clearly defining what physical assets need monitoring, audit existing data collection methods, start small by executing a Proof of Concept (POC) on one dataset at a single location, define a scalable system architecture, add industrial gateways to connect current equipment, centralize data to the intended monitoring server, map telemetry inside the chosen SCADA software, create clear HMI visualizations, and finally establish automated rules and alert triggers to streamline long-term plant operations.
